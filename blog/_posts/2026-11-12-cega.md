The [last post]({{ site.baseurl }}/j-pal/) covered J-PAL. This one covers the Center for Effective Global Action (CEGA), which overlaps with J-PAL in method but leans further into data science, privacy and cost. Everything here comes from CEGA's own website, read in November 2026.

## What CEGA is

CEGA is a research hub headquartered at the University of California, Berkeley. It began in the early 2000s with a small group of Berkeley economists, and its aim is to improve the lives of people living in poverty through rigorous, inclusive and transparent research ([About CEGA](https://cega.berkeley.edu/about/)).

Like J-PAL, it is a network rather than a consultancy:

- **More than 200 faculty** and 100 scholars from low- and middle-income countries, plus hundreds of graduate students ([About CEGA](https://cega.berkeley.edu/about/)).
- **Regional research networks** that invest in local researchers, such as the East Africa Social Science Translation Collaborative, Development Impact West Africa and a fellowship for early-career African researchers ([Global Networks](https://cega.berkeley.edu/global-networks/)).
- **Program areas** that run from agriculture and health to conflict, gender and financial inclusion ([Our Work](https://cega.berkeley.edu/our-work/)). Four of them matter most for allocation decisions: AI and data science, targeting, cost-effectiveness and transparency.

## Upstream: before you build

### Targeting and data science

CEGA's [AI and data science work](https://cega.berkeley.edu/data-science-for-development/) uses satellite imagery, phone data and machine learning to get a more accurate and frequent picture of poverty. Its [Targeting Aid Better](https://cega.berkeley.edu/collection/targeting-aid-better/) initiative helps governments and NGOs use data to decide who receives social protection, with work in Togo, Jordan, Pakistan and Ghana.

The best-known example is Togo's Novissi cash transfers during COVID-19. Satellite poverty maps picked the poorest cantons, and machine learning on phone data estimated which subscribers were poorest. Researchers calculated that the phone-based approach was 42% more precise than simply targeting the 100 poorest cantons ([CEGA](https://cega.berkeley.edu/feature/togo-cash-transfers/)). This is Class 2 in AI4SG Finder's terms: who gets a one-off transfer.

CEGA's tools for this kind of work are free:

- **[Data and toolkits](https://cega.berkeley.edu/our-resources/data/)**, including a global poverty map, a training manual for estimating poverty from satellite images, and guides to sample sizes and running an impact evaluation.
- **[Geo4Dev](https://cega.berkeley.edu/collection/geospatial-analysis-for-development/)**, tutorials and workshops on using satellite and other geospatial data.
- **A brief on explaining AI targeting.** [*Improving the Transparency and Explainability of AI in the Targeting of Aid*](https://cega.berkeley.edu/document/improving-the-transparency-and-explainability-of-ai-in-the-targeting-of-aid/) (November 2025) argues that efficiency is not enough: programs should also reach the most vulnerable, treat people with dignity and obtain informed consent.

### Privacy

CEGA's [Data Privacy Lab](https://cega.berkeley.edu/collection/data-privacy-lab/) builds privacy-enhancing technologies, such as differential privacy and synthetic data, for governments and implementers in low- and middle-income countries. It offers technical assistance, and says its tools are open source and vendor-free. Its projects include protecting refugee microdata for UNHCR.

Most allocation methods need data about individual people. A program planning to share that data with a technical partner should look at this work first. [AI4SG Finder's readiness checks](https://github.com/ashitpatel/ai4sgfinder/blob/main/prompt/knowledge/playbook.md#follow-up-2-readiness-checks) cover the basics, such as replacing names and phone numbers with random IDs, but they don't replace specialist advice.

### Cost

CEGA notes that fewer than one in five impact evaluations include evidence on cost ([Cost-Effectiveness](https://cega.berkeley.edu/cost-effectiveness/)). Its Cost-Effectiveness Lab works on measuring impact per dollar, and its toolkits include a simple cost reporting table and a template for planning cost analysis before a study starts.

This matters for allocation. A model that improves targeting by a few percent may not be worth its build and running costs. AI4SG Finder asks every program to compare the model with a simpler option on its full cost; CEGA's tools help put numbers on that comparison.

## Downstream: when you have a program to test

CEGA's [For Partners](https://cega.berkeley.edu/engage/for-partners/) page is written for governments, NGOs, implementers and companies. Its services include:

- forging research collaborations in low- and middle-income countries;
- finding new uses for existing data, including phone, satellite and network data;
- collecting new data, including costs;
- strengthening monitoring and evaluation;
- designing impact evaluations with the people who will use the results.

The entry point is a **Research Collaboration Form** on that page. CEGA shares it with its network, and if a researcher is interested, sets up a call to discuss questions, methods, timelines and funding. CEGA says it seeks collaborators with flexible scale-up plans, so researchers can help decide when and where a program expands.

As with J-PAL, research funding goes to researchers. CEGA's grants are led by its affiliated faculty ([For Researchers](https://cega.berkeley.edu/engage/for-researchers/)); an NGO comes in as the implementing partner.

CEGA also builds capacity inside government. The [Togo AI Lab](https://cega.berkeley.edu/collection/togo-ai-lab/) builds a data-science team within Togo's government, and reports work with more than ten agencies.

## Allocation decisions CEGA has studied

- **Who gets cash transfers** (Class 2): Togo, above.
- **Which job-search help a refugee gets** (Classes 7 and 8): an adaptive targeted field experiment with refugees in Jordan, listed under [Targeting Aid Better](https://cega.berkeley.edu/collection/targeting-aid-better/), learned which kind of help worked for whom while it ran.
- **Which shipments customs officers inspect** (Class 12): in Zambia, researchers are working with the revenue authority's AI unit to give each incoming shipment a risk score, so officers can focus physical inspections ([project](https://cega.berkeley.edu/collection/leveraging-machine-learning-for-countering-customs-tax-evasion-in-zambia/)). Results are not yet published.

## Where AI4SG Finder fits

CEGA's targeting work is among the strongest evidence behind two of AI4SG Finder's twelve classes. But CEGA, like J-PAL, starts from a program and a research question. It doesn't offer a way to decide which kind of method a new problem needs. Which one to start with depends on what you already know.

**Start with CEGA when you already know your question:**

- **You're deciding who gets a transfer or benefit, at national or regional scale, in a low- or middle-income country, and your data is phone, satellite or survey data.** This is CEGA's core targeting work. Read the Togo work and the [explainable-AI brief](https://cega.berkeley.edu/document/improving-the-transparency-and-explainability-of-ai-in-the-targeting-of-aid/), use the [toolkits](https://cega.berkeley.edu/our-resources/data/) and Geo4Dev, and if you have a government or large-program partner, fill in the collaboration form.
- **Your blocker is sharing sensitive data**, not choosing a method. Contact the [Data Privacy Lab](https://cega.berkeley.edu/collection/data-privacy-lab/) before anything leaves your systems.
- **You already run a program at scale and want to know whether it works, or what it costs per result.** That is an evaluation question. Use the collaboration form, and plan costs with CEGA's cost reporting table.

**Start with AI4SG Finder when you know what you allocate but not how to decide:**

- **You don't yet know which kind of method fits, or whether you need a model at all.** Run the interview. It names the decision, recommends a method family or a simple rule, and sets out the simpler option to beat.
- **Your decision isn't targeting**: routes, patrols, matches, waitlists, repeated visits, inspections. AI4SG Finder covers all twelve kinds and points to build partners and tools for each.
- **You're not ready for an evaluation**: the program is small, can't be staged, or doesn't yet record its outcome. AI4SG Finder's pilot design and build partners come first.

**Then move from one to the other.** After an AI4SG Finder recommendation, CEGA's resources fill specific gaps: privacy safeguards during the readiness checks, cost reporting during the pilot, and, once the program passes the three-part test, the collaboration form, with AI4SG Finder's one-page brief as your first draft. Going the other way, if CEGA's work shows that better targeting is possible but leaves open how to turn it into a rule your staff can run, AI4SG Finder can help you compare that rule with simpler ones and plan how staff would use it.

If your question is about a large program ready for a randomized evaluation in one of J-PAL's sectors, start with J-PAL's regional office instead. Either way, the conversation starts with a researcher, not an institution.

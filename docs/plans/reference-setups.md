# Reference setups: what others published about building and running their tools

Status: done 2026-11-23. The verified findings are in the playbook's [*Others' setups*](../../prompt/knowledge/playbook.md#others-setups-what-has-been-published), under *What it takes* in Follow-up 4.

## Why

The playbook's *What it takes* tells a program what to find out about cost, effort and timeline, and says never to guess figures. Programs also need examples: what a real setup took. This pass looked for deployments that published who built them, the team, how long the build and pilot took, what it cost to build and run, who maintained it, and what happened later.

## How

Four research agents covered three classes each (1, 8, 11; 2, 7, 10; 3, 4, 9; 5, 6, 12), told to report only figures they could quote from a source, with its link, and to write "not published" otherwise. Every figure that went into the playbook was then checked by downloading the source and finding the quoted words in it. Three figures failed that check and were dropped: a US$15,000 prize for the Boston bus routing (not on the cited page), a "five-member" team for Annie MOORE (not in the cited letter), and "pro bono" for Eva in Greece (from a page not checked). Panelot's citizens'-assembly figures were dropped because the source couldn't be opened for checking.

Some sources blocked automated reading and were not used: Nature article pages, some PubMed Central pages, michigan.gov, The New Humanitarian, ReliefWeb and a Tilburg University page.

## What was found

Twenty setups across eleven classes made it into the playbook. The pattern:

- **Durations and team shapes are often published; costs almost never are.** No setup found publishes what its model cost to build. The exceptions are spending on the program rather than the model (OCHA's pilots, GiveDirectly's saving on field staff, Novissi's transfers) and two whole-system figures: Denver's school-choice contract and yearly support cost, and Aarau's participatory-budget accounts, the most complete breakdown found.
- **The handover is the least documented step.** Several setups were built by research teams; what happened after they left is often missing (ARMMAN after 2025, Annie MOORE after 2020, PAWS's planned move into SMART).
- **Flint is the clearest warning.** Model-guided digs found lead in 70% of homes through 2017; after a new contractor set the model aside, 15%.
- **The network-seeding trial in Los Angeles named its own sustainability problem**: mapping each network took the social-work team a week or more, which its authors call unsustainable for an agency alone.

## What is not published anywhere looked

- **Class 1:** build or running cost, team size, ARMMAN's maintenance staffing, and whether the system runs after 2025. 99DOTS was not researched.
- **Class 2:** the cost of building Novissi's targeting; who maintains Panelot.
- **Class 3:** costs; whether PAWS joined SMART; PROTECT after 2013.
- **Class 4:** months and staff to design a trigger framework; model costs separate from cash; an independent evaluation of the 2024 Nigeria pilot.
- **Class 5:** costs for New York's and Boston's school matches and the kidney exchanges; only Denver's are published.
- **Class 6:** running costs and staffing for Wieliczka and the Stanford platform; whether Aarau runs a second round.
- **Class 7:** no NGO or agency has published standing use of effect-based targeting; only research trials (Precision Development's advisory calls in India, financial-aid nudges in New York) were found.
- **Class 8:** what the adaptive algorithms cost, who ran them weekly, and whether IRC or ARMMAN kept them.
- **Class 9:** build and licence costs for Optimus and Boston; no free-tool food-bank routing setup with published team, time or cost.
- **Class 10:** costs for Annie MOORE, GeoMatch and Feeding America; GeoMatch's team size; Annie MOORE after 2020.
- **Class 11:** total costs; any agency taking over the Los Angeles intervention; the outcome of Haryana's planned scale-up.
- **Class 12:** what any model cost to build or maintain; build durations; whether Chicago still runs its models; who maintained Eva after 2020.

## Leads not used

These came up but weren't checked closely enough to cite: Precision Development's learned targeting rules (NBER w34951), GeoMatch's pilots and its offer to support pilots free of charge, the Alliance for Paired Kidney Donation's Matchgrid, the Stanford Participatory Budgeting Platform's use by over 100 local governments, Chicago's lead-poisoning model, the Haryana immunization scale-up with J-PAL South Asia, Novissi's later role in a World Bank project, and IFRC early action protocols. A later pass could check them.

## Keeping it current

Recheck the table with the quarterly scan ([scanning.md](../scanning.md)): links, later fates, and any newly published costs. Prefer sources that report a handover or a stop; they are the rarest and most useful.
